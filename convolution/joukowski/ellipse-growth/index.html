01 · LENGTH AND DIRECTION

Add the arrows, head to tail

Write \(w_j=\alpha_j\alpha_{n-j}\), where \(\alpha_j=\binom{2j}{j}4^{-j}\), and \(m_j=n-2j\). The weights obey \(w_j>0\), \(w_j=w_{n-j}\), and \(\sum_jw_j=1\).

\[P_n(\Gamma_\varrho(\theta))=\sum_{j=0}^n\underbrace{w_j\varrho^{m_j}}_{\text{length}}\underbrace{e^{im_j\theta}}_{\text{direction}}.\]

02 · THE SUPREMUM BOUND

Cancellation, pairing, then the largest frequency

▶ Watch the supremum proof

Set \(s=\log\varrho\). The vector lengths are fixed while \(\theta\) changes. The sum of those lengths is an exact, angle-independent envelope; the theorem bounds that envelope by the extreme frequencies.

1 · Remove cancellation

\[|P_n(\Gamma_\varrho(\theta))|\le\underbrace{\sum_jw_j\varrho^{m_j}}_{S}.\]

A straight line from the origin to the endpoint is no longer than the path along all the arrows. At \(\theta=0\), they align and equality holds.

2 · Pair reciprocal weights

\[S=\tfrac12\sum_jw_j(e^{m_js}+e^{-m_js})\]
\[=\sum_jw_j\cosh(m_js).\]

The partner \(n-j\) has the same weight and the opposite frequency. This equality holds for the sum, not for each original term.

3 · Use the extreme frequencies

\[\cosh(m_js)\le\cosh(ns),\quad |m_j|\le n.\]
\[S\le\underbrace{\cosh(ns)}_B=\frac{\varrho^n+\varrho^{-n}}2.\]

The weights sum to one. A weighted average of these values cannot exceed their largest value.

Follow every colour through the inequalities

The second and third bars have equal total length. Pairing redistributes the coloured parts without changing their total.

From the boundary picture to the supremum norm

The triangle inequality bounds every boundary point. Since \(P_n\) is a polynomial, the maximum-modulus principle gives the same supremum over the ellipse. Moreover, \(\theta=0\) attains \(S\), so

\[\|P_n\|_{A(E_\varrho)}=\|P_n\|_{H^\infty(E_\varrho)}=P_n\!\left(\frac{\varrho+\varrho^{-1}}2\right)=S\le B.\]

For \(n=0\) or \(n=1\), \(S=B\). For \(n\ge2\) and \(\varrho>1\), the positive weights at inner frequencies make \(S<B\). At the radius-one limiting trace, both equal one.

03 · AVERAGE SQUARED SIZE

The Hardy norm measures a whole turn

▶ Watch the Hardy proof

The supremum measures the largest value. The Hardy norm measures the root-mean-square value, using uniform angle \(d\theta/(2\pi)\), not arclength on the ellipse.

The curve is sampled for drawing. The mean is evaluated from all Fourier coefficients, not estimated from the plotted samples.

Orthogonality removes cross terms

When a full turn is averaged, different integer frequencies cancel against one another. Parseval leaves the sum of the squared arrow lengths:

\[\|P_n\|_{H^2(E_\varrho)}^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|P_n(\Gamma_\varrho(\theta))|^2\,d\theta=\sum_jw_j^2\varrho^{2m_j}.\]

Now use \(w_j^2\le w_j\) and the same reciprocal pairing, with \(2s\) in place of \(s\):

\[\sum_jw_j^2\varrho^{2m_j}\le\sum_jw_j\varrho^{2m_j}=\sum_jw_j\cosh(2m_js)\le\cosh(2ns).\]
\[\|P_n\|_{H^2(E_\varrho)}\le\sqrt{\cosh(2ns)}=\sqrt{\frac{\varrho^{2n}+\varrho^{-2n}}2}.\]

Squared quantities · one shared scale

Each colour begins with the energy of one Fourier coefficient. This is a sum of squares, not the square of the sum of lengths.

Source, normalisation and limits of this illustration

This lesson follows Subsection 3.2.3 — Geometric growth of Legendre modes on an ellipse and Lemma 3.19 in Section 3.2 of the thesis. Its cited source is Bandtlow’s EDMD–Legendre Lemma 1.6; the two lemma numbers refer to different documents.

The theorem assumes \(\varrho>1\) and the unnormalised Legendre polynomial \(P_n\). The control also includes \(\varrho=1\) to show the limiting real interval. This degenerate trace is not a Hardy ellipse.

For \(\widetilde P_n=\sqrt{(2n+1)/2}\,P_n\), both norm bounds receive the same explicit factor. The switch scales the diagrams and readouts; the proof formulas above retain the base mode \(P_n\).

The calculations illustrate the finite Laurent identity and are checked against the independent three-term recurrence. Numerical diagnostics are not a replacement for the displayed proof.
