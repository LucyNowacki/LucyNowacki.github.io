Follow the kernel from circle to contour

How does each surviving term rotate?

\(Q_n(\Gamma_\varrho(\theta))=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{n,k_q}\varrho^{-k_q}e^{-ik_q\theta}\)
\(k_q=n+1+2q.\)

The \(P_n\) packet is finite, with frequencies from \(-n\) to \(+n\) in steps of two. The \(Q_n\) tail starts one step farther out and continues clockwise through frequencies \(-(n+1),-(n+3),\ldots\).

What changes when we pull the kernel back?

Start with \(Q_n(\omega)\), defined off the red interval \([-1,1]\). Choose a point \(z\) outside the unit circle and use \(J(z)=\tfrac12(z+z^{-1})\) as its address in the \(\omega\)-plane. Reading \(Q_n\) at that address defines the pull-back \(z\mapsto Q_n(J(z))\). Fixing \(|z|=\varrho\) leaves just one input, the angle \(\theta\).

Compare the Laurent series on the two sheets

The outer rim of \(\varrho^{-1}<|z|<\varrho\) traces the coefficient contour once. Its reciprocal partner \(s=1/z\) gives the same kernel value at the same image point. Substituting \(z=1/s\) explains the change of powers across the two sheets.

\[\begin{aligned} Q_n(J(z))&=\sum_{q\ge0}\sigma_{n,k_q}z^{-k_q}, &&|z|>1,\\ Q_n(J(s))&=\sum_{q\ge0}\sigma_{n,k_q}s^{k_q}, &&0<|s|<1. \end{aligned}\]

The two expansions meet the excluded interval at the unit circle. On the chosen outer rim each term has radial factor \(\varrho^{-k_q}\).

Evaluate a function

\[Q_n(\omega)=Q_n(J(z))=Q_n(\Gamma_\varrho(\theta)).\]

These three readings agree at the linked points. Changing coordinates alone leaves the complex value unchanged.

Transport a contour differential

\[Q_n(\omega)\,d\omega=\underbrace{Q_n(J(z))J'(z)}_{\widetilde Q_n(z)}\,dz.\]

The complex Jacobian \(J'(z)=\tfrac12(1-z^{-2})\) scales a small displacement by \(|J'(z)|\) and turns it through \(\arg J'(z)\). Its direction matters in a contour integral.

From oriented tangent to angular energy

Along the circle, \(dz=iz\,d\theta\), so the contour density satisfies \(\widetilde Q_n(z)\,iz=Q_n(\Gamma_\varrho(\theta))\,\Gamma_\varrho'(\theta)\). For the contour integral we keep the full vector \(d\omega=\Gamma'_\varrho(\theta)\,d\theta\). Cauchy–Schwarz then asks for the squared magnitude of \(Q_n(\Gamma_\varrho)\Gamma'_\varrho\), averaged in \(d\theta/(2\pi)\). This is where the squared speed enters.

\[\begin{aligned}\Gamma'_\varrho(\theta)&=\tfrac i2(\varrho e^{i\theta}-\varrho^{-1}e^{-i\theta}),\\[3pt] |\Gamma'_\varrho(\theta)|^2&=\tfrac14(\varrho^2+\varrho^{-2}-e^{2i\theta}-e^{-2i\theta})\\ &=a-\tfrac12\cos(2\theta),\quad a=\tfrac14(\varrho^2+\varrho^{-2}).\end{aligned}\]

What survives a full turn?

The dot follows \(\theta\). The dashed line is the full-turn mean. The energy views use the \(L\) retained terms \(Q_{n,L}=\sum_k c_k e^{-ik\theta}\), with \(c_k=\sigma_{n,k}\varrho^{-k}\).

Parseval’s role. In the unweighted mean, \(\langle e^{i(\ell-k)\theta}\rangle=0\) unless \(k=\ell\). Hence \(\langle |Q_{n,L}|^2\rangle=\sum_k c_k^2\). The squared speed adds frequencies \(0,\pm2\). After multiplication a pair can have zero total frequency when \(\ell-k=0\) or \(\pm2\). Each ordered neighbour contributes \(-c_kc_\ell/4\).

Why only this ladder remains

Worked calculations

Expand the Cauchy kernel after the Joukowski substitution. The surviving coefficients are ordinary real integrals, and their exact zeros follow from degree and parity.

\[\sigma_{n,k}=\int_0^\pi P_n(\cos t)\sin(kt)\,dt=\int_{-1}^{1}P_n(x)U_{k-1}(x)\,dx\quad(k\ge1).\]

Here \(U_{k-1}\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind. This formula follows from \(\sin(kt)=\sin t\,U_{k-1}(\cos t)\).

1 · Remove lower degrees

For \(1\le k\le n\), \(U_{k-1}\) has degree less than \(n\). Legendre orthogonality therefore gives \(\sigma_{n,k}=0\). Also \(\sigma_{n,0}=0\) directly.

\[k\le n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sigma_{n,k}=0.\]

2 · Remove the matching parity

Reflecting \(t\) to \(\pi-t\) gives \(\sigma_{n,k}=(-1)^{n+k+1}\sigma_{n,k}\). When \(n\) and \(k\) have the same parity, this forces a zero.

\[k=n+1,n+3,n+5,\ldots\]

The remaining indices have the opposite parity to \(n\).

3 · Bound the surviving weights

The non-negative cosine packet for \(P_n(\cos t)\) makes each surviving contribution non-negative. The real-line bound \(|P_n(x)|\le1\) and \(\int_0^\pi|\sin(kt)|dt=2\) give

\[0\le\sigma_{n,k}\le2.\]

The first possible coefficient \(\sigma_{n,n+1}\) is strictly positive.

Decay comes from the radius

On \(z=\varrho e^{i\theta}\), the unit factor \(e^{-ik\theta}\) controls direction, while \(\varrho^{-k}\) shrinks the length. The powers along the ladder have ratio \(\varrho^{-2}\). The coefficients themselves need not have a constant ratio.

\[|Q_n(\Gamma_\varrho(\theta))|\le2\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\varrho^{-(n+1+2q)}=\frac{2\varrho^{-n}}{\varrho-\varrho^{-1}}.\]

Increasing \(n\) moves the first surviving power farther down the tail. This is where the factor \(\varrho^{-n}\) in the coefficient estimate comes from. For \(f\in A(E_\varrho)\), insert the envelope into the contour integral for \(a_n(f)\) and multiply by the boundary length.

\[|a_n(f)|\le\frac{(2n+1)|\partial E_\varrho|}{\pi(\varrho-\varrho^{-1})}\max_{\partial E_\varrho}|f|\,\varrho^{-n}.\]

The pulled-back contour also needs its Jacobian

\[\widetilde Q_n(z)=Q_n(\operatorname{Jouk}(z))\,\frac12(1-z^{-2}).\]

The displayed arrows sum \(Q_n(\operatorname{Jouk}(z))\). The actual circle-contour kernel includes \(\operatorname{Jouk}'(z)\), so its coefficients are shifted differences and are not the same non-negative \(\sigma_{n,k}\).

Source, convergence and numerical meaning

This is Subsection 3.2.5, Lemma 3.24, of the integrated thesis. Its cited source is Bandtlow's EDMD–Legendre Lemma 1.8. The series converges absolutely and uniformly on every fixed circle \(|z|=\varrho>1\).

The coefficients use the classical \(P_n(1)=1\) convention. The code evaluates the finite positive cosine-packet expression for \(\sigma_{n,k}\); structural degree and parity zeros are imposed exactly. All nonzero readouts use floating-point arithmetic.

\(B_L\) bounds the omitted series in exact arithmetic, uniformly in \(\theta\). It does not include floating-point rounding error. The plotted endpoint is \(Q_{n,L}\), not the infinite sum.
