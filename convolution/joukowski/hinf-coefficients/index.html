THESIS WALKTHROUGH · SUBSECTION 3.2.6 · LEMMA 3.25

Analyticity forces coefficient decay

On each inner contour \(\partial E_s\) with \(1<s<\rho\), the second-kind kernel decays geometrically with degree. The contour formula transfers that decay to the Legendre coefficient, using the size of the function on the outer analytic domain \(E_\rho\).

\[|a_n(f)|\le(2n+1)C_\infty(\varrho)\,\|f\|_{H^\infty(E_\varrho)}\,\varrho^{-n}.\]

The proof in three steps

1 · Start strictly inside

Choose \(1<s<\varrho\). The function is continuous on the smaller closed ellipse, so the contour formula applies there. Its real-line coefficient is still the same \(a_n(f)\).

\[\|f\|_{A(E_s)}\le M_\varrho:=\|f\|_{H^\infty(E_\varrho)}.\]
Watch this proof step

2 · Add absolute contributions

The triangle inequality replaces the complex differential by arclength. Bound the function by \(M_\varrho\) and the kernel by its geometric tail.

\[|a_n(f)|\le\frac{2n+1}{2\pi}|\partial E_s|M_\varrho\sup_{\partial E_s}|Q_n|.\]
\[\sup_{\partial E_s}|Q_n|\le\frac{2s^{-n}}{s-s^{-1}}.\]
Watch this proof step

3 · Approach the outer radius

Let \(s\uparrow\varrho\). The perimeter and the explicit kernel bound depend continuously on the radius. A continuous trace of a general bounded holomorphic function on the outer boundary is not required.

\[C_\infty(\varrho)=\frac{|\partial E_\varrho|}{\pi(\varrho-\varrho^{-1})}.\]
Watch this proof step

Where the earlier lessons enter the proof

The lemma numbers below follow Bandtlow’s preprint. Its Lemma 1.9 is Lemma 3.25 in the thesis.

  1. Lesson 1 · Lemma 1.1 supplies the positive Laurent weights used to determine the signs of the surviving kernel coefficients.
  2. Lesson 2 · Lemma 1.2 bounds the Legendre modes by one on the interval. This gives the upper bound of two on the kernel coefficients and later controls the polynomial tail on the interval.
  3. Lesson 3 · Lemma 1.6(a) controls how the polynomials grow on a smaller ellipse when we reconstruct the function from its coefficients.
  4. Lesson 4 · Lemma 1.7 writes the coefficient as an integral on any strictly inner ellipse.
  5. Lesson 5 · Lemma 1.8 gives the kernel’s one-sided expansion on the exterior circle. Its first power and its spacing by two give the geometric ceiling in Lemma 1.9.

Which ellipse supplies each power?

Choose the violet ellipse \(E_\rho\) on which \(f\) is bounded and analytic. Choose a smaller gold ellipse \(E_r\) on which to approximate it. The radii belong to the circles before the Joukowski map. The two image ellipses have foci at \(-1\) and \(1\).

Gold · Lesson 3 · Bandtlow’s Lemma 1.6(a)

Evaluating the basis on a larger target allows higher modes to be larger.

\[\sup_{E_r}|P_n|\leq \frac{r^n+r^{-n}}{2}\leq r^n.\]
Violet · Lesson 6 · Bandtlow’s Lemma 1.9

The outer ellipse supplies the coefficient decay. The one-sided kernel modes from Lemma 1.8 shrink on the preimage circles as the contour moves outward.

\[|a_n(f)|\leq K_\rho(2n+1)\rho^{-n},\qquad K_\rho=C_\infty(\rho)M_\rho.\]

Multiply the estimates. The degree-\(n\) term carries \(r^n\rho^{-n}=(r/\rho)^n\). For each fixed \(1<r<\rho\), the entire omitted tail tends to zero as \(N\) increases.

\[\sup_{E_r}|f-S_N|\leq K_\rho\sum_{n=N}^\infty(2n+1)(r/\rho)^n \xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0,\qquad S_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_n(f)P_n.\]

How many terms guarantee the same accuracy?

The film fixes the violet outer ellipse \(E_{2.15}\), where \(f(\omega)=1/(2.5-\omega)\) is bounded and analytic. The cyan curve bounds the error everywhere in \(E_{1.29}\), and the pink curve bounds it everywhere in \(E_{1.935}\). Both target boundaries lie strictly inside \(E_{2.15}\), so continuity extends each estimate to its target boundary. Here the gold rim selects one target at a time. Fix both domains and increase \(N\) to compare the bound with the tolerance \(0.01\). Hear which ellipse each curve describes.

To change the denominator alone, choose the smaller gold target \(r=1.29\) and switch the violet radius from \(1.72\) to \(2.15\). Then \(q\) falls from \(0.75\) to \(0.60\). The geometric factor decays faster. The prefactor \(K_\rho\) can also change. A new outer radius is admissible only while \(f\) stays bounded and analytic on its ellipse.

How the bound is calculated

Both targets use the same elementary perimeter estimate from the film. With \(q=r/\rho\) and \(K_* =M_\rho(\rho+\rho^{-1})/(\rho-\rho^{-1})\), the displayed upper bound is

\[B_N=K_*q^N\left(\frac{2N+1}{1-q}+\frac{2q}{(1-q)^2}\right).\]

At the film’s outer radius \(2.15\), this gives \(K_*\approx 1.30162\). The count is the first \(N\) for which this upper bound falls below \(0.01\). The actual error may be smaller. For this rational function every finite partial sum has positive uniform error. The infinite series equals \(f\) on each smaller ellipse.

Source, normalization and numerical meaning

This lesson follows Subsection 3.2.6 — \(H^\infty\) coefficient bounds from the second-kind kernel and Lemma 3.25, in thesis Section 3.2. Bandtlow’s original result is EDMD–Legendre Lemma 1.9.

The normalization is \(P_n(1)=1\) and \(a_n(f)=\frac{2n+1}{2}\int_{-1}^1 f(x)P_n(x)\,dx\). All ellipse radii exceed one.

The plotted function is analytic beyond every displayed closed ellipse. Its pole stays at \(2.5\), and its outer norm is the closed expression shown above. Its coefficients are evaluated using 80 positive Laurent terms at \(z=2.5+\sqrt{5.25}\), so they do not depend on the displayed ellipse radius.

The perimeter option uses 2,048-point angular quadrature. It illustrates the exact perimeter formula but is not an interval enclosure. The explicit fallback uses the analytic estimate

\[|\partial E_\varrho|\le\pi(\varrho+\varrho^{-1}),\qquad C_\infty(\varrho)\le\frac{\varrho+\varrho^{-1}}{\varrho-\varrho^{-1}}.\]

At the final slider position, the inner curve displays the limiting expression \(s\uparrow\varrho\). Between integer degrees, hover values interpolate the plotted curves; they are not coefficients of noninteger Legendre modes. All computed values use ordinary floating-point arithmetic.
