Why the Hilbert-space estimate works

1 · Separate two boundary energies

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the contour formula with normalized angular measure. One factor is the Hardy norm of the function. The other is the weighted kernel energy.

\[|a_n(f)|^2\le(2n+1)^2\|f\|_{H^2(E_R)}^2q_{n,R}.\]

The factor \(|\Gamma'_R|^2\) belongs inside \(q_{n,R}\). The Hardy norm itself uses \(d\theta/(2\pi)\), not arclength.

2 · Keep only allowed interactions

The kernel uses one parity ladder. The boundary weight has only frequencies \(0,2,-2\), so it couples a coefficient only with itself and its two neighbours.

\[|\Gamma'_R(\theta)|^2=\tfrac14(R^2+R^{-2}-e^{2i\theta}-e^{-2i\theta}).\]

All surviving kernel coefficients are nonnegative. Their neighbour correction is therefore subtracted; discarding it gives an upper bound. Then use \(\sigma_{n,k}\le2\) to sum a geometric series.

3 · Extend, then restrict inward

First prove the coefficient bound for functions holomorphic inside the ellipse and continuous on its closure, \(f\in A(E_R)\). Density extends the bounded coefficient functional to all of \(H^2(E_R)\).

On a smaller ellipse, the mode-growth estimate pays at most \(r^n\), while the coefficient contributes \(R^{-n}\). The remaining factor is \((r/R)^n\), with \(r/R<1\).

\[R^{-n}r^n=(r/R)^n.\]
Source, normalization and numerical meaning

This lesson follows Subsection 3.2.7 — \(H^2\) coefficient bound and bridge to projection tails and Lemma 3.28, in thesis Section 3.2. Bandtlow’s original result is EDMD–Legendre Lemma 1.10.

The polynomials satisfy \(P_n(1)=1\). The Hardy norm is the angular norm \(\|f\|_{H^2(E_R)}^2=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(\Gamma_R(\theta))|^2\,d\theta\). For a general Hardy element, the coefficient functional is defined by continuous extension; it agrees with the real-line coefficient of its canonical holomorphic representative.

The kernel graph uses the displayed finite number \(L\) of Laurent terms. It shows the exact finite-sum identity evaluated in floating-point arithmetic, alongside an analytic truncation estimate. A finite weighted kernel energy is not assumed to lie above or below the full infinite energy. The estimate does not enclose floating-point rounding.

The projection graph shows an envelope for functions of unit outer Hardy norm, not actual coefficients or an actual error. It retains modes \(0,\ldots,N-1\). The displayed tail value sums all omitted envelope terms through infinity, even though the chart displays only finitely many bars.

This \(\mathcal P_N\) is the Legendre truncation defined by real-line coefficients. It is not the orthogonal projection for the Hardy inner product. The smaller-radius estimate concerns the canonical holomorphic representative on \(E_r\).
