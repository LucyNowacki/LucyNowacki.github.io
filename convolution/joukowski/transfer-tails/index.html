12 · TRANSFER GEOMETRY AND TAILS

Generic consequences carried into the operator framework

The transfer operator samples the input on a smaller branch-image ellipse, builds its response on a larger ellipse, and returns to the original Hardy space. Each strict gap supplies a geometric decay ratio.

Write \(s=r_\tau(\rho)\) and \(X=H^2(E_r)\), with \(1<s<r<\rho\). The larger of \(s/r\) and \(r/\rho\) gives the slower geometric factor.

Earlier ingredients. The factorisation overview separates operators on whole functions from inverse-branch maps on points. Hardy to uniform controls the input restriction, and uniform to Hardy controls the output restriction.

Intuitive/geometric explanation

The containment \(\tau_b(\overline{E_\rho})\subset E_s\) places every branch evaluation where the restricted input is uniformly controlled. The illustrative branches are \(\tau_\pm(z)=(z\pm1)/2\), with weights \(1/2\). Their response is

\[h(z)=\tfrac12f(\tau_-(z))+\tfrac12f(\tau_+(z)).\]

The point maps move \(z\) inward to two sampling points. The transfer operator combines the sampled values into the new function \(h\) on the outer ellipse. Restricting \(h\) back to \(E_r\) gives the output in the same space \(X\).

The two humps show local input and output energies. Lighthouses show local moduli, with the corresponding global norm readings kept separate. Their inward stems are magnitude gauges. They do not trace the function through the ellipse.

Why the estimate follows

For holomorphic branches and weights on a neighbourhood of \(\overline{E_\rho}\), together with the stated containment, the three steps are

\[X\xrightarrow{\mathcal J_2^{r\to s}}H^\infty(E_s),\]
\[H^\infty(E_s)\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathcal L}_{\rho,\tau}}A(E_\rho),\]
\[A(E_\rho)\xrightarrow{\mathcal J_\infty^{\rho\to r}}X.\]

The generic Hardy-to-uniform bound supplies \(C_2(r)\mathsf S_\infty(s/r)\) on the input leg. The sum of the outer suprema of the branch weights bounds the middle map, and the output restriction has norm one. These bounds establish a bounded factorisation before approximation is introduced.

\[\begin{aligned}q_{\rm gap}&=s/r,\\q_{\rm out}&=r/\rho,\\q_\ast&=\max\{q_{\rm gap},q_{\rm out}\}.\end{aligned}\]

The plotted powers \(q_{\rm gap}^N\) and \(q_{\rm out}^N\) display geometric factors. A full operator-error estimate also contains constants, approximation prefactors, coordinate geometry, and collocation terms. Equalizing the ratios at \(r=\sqrt{s\rho}\), for fixed admissible \(s,\rho\), balances these factors alone.

Connection to the thesis

This follows “Generic consequences carried into the operator framework,” the final subsection of “Two Hardy embeddings and Legendre projection tails.” It passes the restriction and coefficient ingredients to the single-space chapter.

The preparatory input starts in \(H^2(E_\eta)\), giving \(q_{\rm in}=s/\eta\). The single-space theorem controls every \(f\in X=H^2(E_r)\), which need not extend to \(E_\eta\). Its input ratio is therefore \(q_{\rm gap}=s/r\). This change of source space does not identify \(\eta\) with \(r\).

The single-space input remainder uses \(I-\mathcal P_N^X\), with \(\mathcal P_N^X\) the Hardy orthogonal projector. The later Chebyshev-packet estimate handles this projector. The earlier real-line truncation \(\mathcal P_N\) cannot simply be substituted into that argument.

Under the later factorisation and collocation hypotheses, including the subexponential prefactor condition, the convergence result gives \(O(q_0^N)\) for every \(q_\ast<q_0<1\). The endpoint \(O(q_\ast^N)\) follows in the additional regime \(q_{\rm gap}>q_{\rm out}\), using the packet input bound and the strict separation of the two rates. The endpoint is not asserted generally, and neither statement proves optimality.
