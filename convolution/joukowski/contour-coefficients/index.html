Recover a coefficient from the boundary

The preceding lesson controlled how Legendre modes grow on an ellipse. We now move the coefficient integral from the real interval to the ellipse boundary, where the holomorphic extension of the function becomes useful.

\[a_n(f)=\frac{2n+1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial E_\varrho}f(\varpi)Q_n(\varpi)\,d\varpi.\]

Assume \(\varrho>1\) and \(f\in A(E_\varrho)\): the function is holomorphic inside the ellipse and continuous on its closure. The contour is positively oriented.

Why the boundary gives the same coefficient

First recover each value on the real interval by Cauchy's formula. Then combine those values using the Legendre weights. Interchanging the two integrations reveals a single boundary kernel.

1 · Recover one real-line value

\[f(x)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial E_\varrho}\frac{f(\varpi)}{\varpi-x}\,d\varpi.\]

Every \(x\in[-1,1]\) lies strictly inside the ellipse. The “One real-line value” setting shows this simpler contour identity.

2 · Apply the Legendre weight

\[a_n(f)=\left(n+\frac12\right)\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)P_n(x)\,dx.\]

Insert the Cauchy representation of \(f(x)\). The contour stays a positive distance from the interval, so the parametrised integrand is continuous on a compact rectangle and Fubini applies.

3 · Collect the Cauchy kernels

\[Q_n(\omega)=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_n(x)}{\omega-x}\,dx,\qquad\omega\notin[-1,1].\]

The real integration produces \(2Q_n(\varpi)\), leaving the stated contour formula. This is Cauchy's formula followed by Fubini, rather than a substitution from the interval to the ellipse.

The differential carries direction

\[\varpi=\Gamma_\varrho(\theta),\qquad d\varpi=\Gamma'_\varrho(\theta)\,d\theta.\]

The complex tangent multiplies the integrand and turns its direction as the boundary is traversed. Its modulus appears only when taking an arclength estimate. In the interactive, the tangent arrow is rescaled to show direction, not speed.

Source, normalisation and numerical meaning

The definition is Definition 3.21; the coefficient identity is Lemma 3.22 in Subsection 3.2.4 of the integrated thesis. The cited preprint calls the identity Bandtlow's EDMD–Legendre Lemma 1.7.

The convention is the classical Legendre polynomial \(P_n(1)=1\). For the initial example, \(\omega^2=\tfrac13P_0(\omega)+\tfrac23P_2(\omega)\), so the exact reference is \(a_2=2/3\). Changing the contour radius does not change that coefficient.

The contour curve uses 1,024 midpoint quadrature panels and 160 terms of the kernel series. The real coefficient for \(e^\omega\) is independently evaluated by real-line quadrature. These are floating-point illustrations, with no interval certificate.
