Recover a coefficient from the boundary

The preceding lesson controlled how Legendre modes grow on an ellipse. We now move the coefficient integral from the real interval to the ellipse boundary, where the holomorphic extension of the function becomes useful.

\[a_n(f)=\frac{2n+1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial E_\varrho}f(\varpi)Q_n(\varpi)\,d\varpi.\]

Assume \(\varrho>1\) and \(f\in A(E_\varrho)\): the function is holomorphic inside the ellipse and continuous on its closure. The contour is positively oriented.

How does a moving tangent give one quarter?

Fix \(f(\varpi)=\varpi^2\) and \(x=\tfrac12\). Follow one point on the contour and the value it adds to the integral.

Which part survives at the pole?

\(\displaystyle F(\varpi)=\frac{\varpi^2}{\varpi-x}=(\varpi-x)+2x+\frac{x^2}{\varpi-x}.\)

Multiplying by the distance vector \(\varpi-x\) removes the denominator. Bring the gold point closer to the pink ring from any angle. Its squared value approaches the coefficient of the reciprocal term, called the residue.

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Res}_{\varpi=x}F=\lim_{\varpi\to x}(\varpi-x)F(\varpi)=x^2=\tfrac14.\)

Join the two boundary points at the same angle

\(\displaystyle S(t)=x+\varepsilon e^{it},\qquad \Gamma_2(t)=\tfrac54\cos t+i\tfrac34\sin t.\)
\(\displaystyle C_\lambda(t)=(1-\lambda)S(t)+\lambda\Gamma_2(t),\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq1.\)

At a fixed angle, the gold point divides the dotted segment in the fraction \(\lambda\). At zero it is on the small circle, at one it is on the ellipse. Each intermediate loop goes counterclockwise around the pole once. Changing the loop changes the partial trace, while every full trace ends at the same diamond.

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_\lambda}F(\varpi)\,d\varpi=\tfrac14.\)

Turn the contour tangent into the integral’s next direction

\(\displaystyle d\varpi=C_\lambda'(t)\,dt,\qquad A_C'(t)=\frac{F(C_\lambda(t))}{2\pi i}\,C_\lambda'(t).\)

The cyan arrow points along the input contour. Multiplication by the complex number \(F(C_\lambda)/(2\pi i)\) rotates and scales it into the pink arrow on the accumulated trace. Each arrow shows a tangent times the same short angle \(h\). The curved pieces show the actual motion over \(h\). Reduce \(h\) to compare.

Watch the three contributions to the integral

\(\displaystyle \varpi=x+\varepsilon e^{it},\qquad \frac{d\varpi}{\varpi-x}=\frac{i\varepsilon e^{it}\,dt}{\varepsilon e^{it}}=i\,dt.\)
\(\displaystyle A_C(T)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^T\!\left(\underbrace{\tfrac14}_{\rm constant}+\tfrac14e^{it}+\tfrac1{16}e^{2it}\right)dt.\)

The constant carries the endpoint steadily to the right. The other two integrals travel around circles once and twice, respectively. At a full turn they both return to zero. The gold tip adds the three coloured displacements head to tail.

\(\displaystyle A_C(0)=0,\qquad A_C(2\pi)=\tfrac14+0+0=\tfrac14.\)

Why the boundary gives the same coefficient

First recover each value on the real interval by Cauchy's formula. Then combine those values using the Legendre weights. Interchanging the two integrations reveals a single boundary kernel.

1 · Recover one real-line value

\[f(x)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial E_\varrho}\frac{f(\varpi)}{\varpi-x}\,d\varpi.\]

Every \(x\in[-1,1]\) lies strictly inside the ellipse. Take x = ½ in the film example. The small circle and the ellipse both recover ¼.

▶ Watch one real-line value

2 · Apply the Legendre weight

\[a_n(f)=\left(n+\frac12\right)\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)P_n(x)\,dx.\]

Insert the Cauchy representation of \(f(x)\). The contour stays a positive distance from the interval, so the parametrised integrand is continuous on a compact rectangle and Fubini applies.

▶ Watch the Legendre weight

3 · Collect the Cauchy kernels

\[\begin{gathered}Q_n(\omega)=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_n(x)}{\omega-x}\,dx,\\ \omega\notin[-1,1].\end{gathered}\]

The real integration produces \(2Q_n(\varpi)\), leaving the stated contour formula. This is Cauchy's formula followed by Fubini, rather than a substitution from the interval to the ellipse.

▶ Watch the kernels combine

The differential carries direction

\[\varpi=\Gamma_\varrho(\theta),\qquad d\varpi=\Gamma'_\varrho(\theta)\,d\theta.\]

The complex tangent multiplies the integrand and turns its direction as the boundary is traversed. Its modulus appears only when taking an arclength estimate. On the ellipse with semiaxes a and b, the tangent is −a sin θ + i b cos θ. It points up at the right tip and left at the top. The same arrow scale throughout a circuit lets you compare the speeds there.

Source, normalisation and numerical meaning

The definition is Definition 3.21; the coefficient identity is Lemma 3.22 in Subsection 3.2.4 of the integrated thesis. The cited preprint calls the identity Bandtlow's EDMD–Legendre Lemma 1.7.

The convention is the classical Legendre polynomial \(P_n(1)=1\). For the initial example, \(\omega^2=\tfrac13P_0(\omega)+\tfrac23P_2(\omega)\), so the exact reference is \(a_2=2/3\). Changing the contour radius does not change that coefficient.

For the stated boundary regularity, apply Cauchy and Fubini on an inner ellipse \(E_s\), with \(1<s<\varrho\), then let \(s\uparrow\varrho\). Continuity up to the boundary and separation from the interval give the limit.

The contour curve uses 1,024 midpoint quadrature panels and 160 terms of the kernel series. The real coefficient for \(e^\omega\) is independently evaluated by real-line quadrature. These are floating-point illustrations, with no interval certificate.
