Measure an arc around the probe

What to try. Move the probe around the ellipse and compare local energy with the full Hardy norm. Then increase \(N\), keeping the radii fixed, to compare the actual polynomial remainder with its upper bound.

Earlier ingredients. Hardy coefficients gives the coefficient estimate. Geometric growth and weighted synthesis turn it into uniform control.

Intuitive/geometric explanation

The nine-term example starts from \(p=\sum_{n=0}^{8}0.45^nP_n\) and sets \(f=Ap/\|p\|_{\mathrm{source}}\). The source norm is the one shown in the first stage. The constant option uses \(f\equiv A\).

Restriction keeps the same holomorphic function and changes where it is measured. The outer hump shows the local energy \(|f(\Gamma_R(t))|^2\). Its normalized angular mean is the squared source norm. The inner lighthouse shows \(|f(\Gamma_r(t))|\), and the target norm is the supremum over the smaller ellipse.

\[\|f\|_{H^2(E_R)}^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(\Gamma_R(t))|^2\,dt.\]

Here \(\Gamma_R(t)=(Re^{it}+R^{-1}e^{-it})/2\). This is an average in the Joukowski angle, rather than in arclength. For a general Hardy element, the boundary expression is understood through the Hardy completion. Polynomial examples have ordinary continuous boundary values.

A local value or a finite polynomial example is not an operator norm. In this direction, the restriction need not be a contraction. The available bound accounts for both the source radius and its gap from the target.

Why the estimate follows

Write \(q=r/R\) and

\[C_2(R)=\left(\frac{R^2+R^{-2}}{R^2-R^{-2}}\right)^{1/2}.\]

The Hardy coefficient estimate is \(|a_n(f)|\le(2n+1)C_2(R)\|f\|_{H^2(E_R)}R^{-n}\). Multiply by \(\|P_n\|_{A(E_r)}\le r^n\), then sum the full series, its prefix, or its tail.

\[\begin{aligned}\|\mathcal J_2^{R\to r}\|&\le C_2(R)\mathsf S_\infty(q),\\\|\mathcal P_N\|&\le C_2(R)\mathsf S_N(q),\\\|\mathcal J_2^{R\to r}-\mathcal P_N\|&\le C_2(R)\mathsf T_N(q).\end{aligned}\]

All three operator norms here run from \(H^2(E_R)\) to \(H^\infty(E_r)\). Neumann reconstruction identifies the sum with the canonical holomorphic representative of \(f\). The scalar sums are exact, while these are upper bounds.

For a fixed strict gap, \(\mathsf T_N(q)\to0\). Since \(\mathcal P_N\) has finite rank, the restriction is compact. The argument requires \(r<R\).

Connection to the thesis

This follows “The generic embedding \(\mathcal J_2^{R\to r}:H^2(E_R)\hookrightarrow H^\infty(E_r)\)” in the Distilled section “Two Hardy embeddings and Legendre projection tails.” The manuscript organizes the earlier Hardy-to-uniform mechanism by exact scalar sums and refines its coarse tail bound.

In the later transfer factorisation, this restriction reaches the smaller ellipse containing the inverse-branch images. The single-space input remainder instead uses \(\mathcal J_2^{r\to s}(I-\mathcal P_N^X)\), where \(X=H^2(E_r)\). Its Hardy orthogonal projector requires the later Chebyshev-packet argument. Replacing the real-line \(\mathcal P_N\) in the generic tail by \(\mathcal P_N^X\) would leave a proof obligation.
