Measure an arc around the probe

What to try. Compare the source supremum with the target Hardy norm while moving the probe. Then increase \(N\) and compare the projection remainder with its coefficient-based tail bound. The restriction and the approximation answer different questions.

Earlier ingredients. The normalized Hardy average gives the restriction estimate directly. Bounded analytic coefficients, mode growth, and weighted synthesis are needed for the projection bounds.

Intuitive/geometric explanation

The nine-term example starts from \(p=\sum_{n=0}^{8}0.45^nP_n\) and sets \(f=Ap/\|p\|_{\mathrm{source}}\). The source norm is the one shown in the first stage. The constant option uses \(f\equiv A\).

Let \(M=\|f\|_{H^\infty(E_R)}\). Every point of the inner boundary lies inside the source ellipse, so its energy is at most \(M^2\). Averaging those energies with total weight one cannot exceed the same ceiling.

\[\|\mathcal J_\infty^{R\to r}f\|_{H^2(E_r)}^2\le M^2.\]

The constant function \(f\equiv1\) attains equality, proving \(\|\mathcal J_\infty^{R\to r}\|=1\). A nonconstant illustrative polynomial will usually give a smaller ratio. The moving hump displays local energy, and the Hardy norm uses its full angular average followed by a square root.

Both generic embeddings restrict to a smaller ellipse. They differ in their source and target norms, and are not inverse operations. The outer-boundary lighthouse is available for the continuous polynomial example. General \(H^\infty\) functions are defined by their supremum inside the open source ellipse.

Why the estimate follows

For the projection bounds, put \(q=r/R\) and

\[C_\infty(R)=\frac{|\partial E_R|}{\pi(R-R^{-1})}.\]

The numerator is the geometric perimeter. The coefficient estimate \(|a_n(f)|\le(2n+1)C_\infty(R)\|f\|_{H^\infty(E_R)}R^{-n}\), followed by mode growth and synthesis, gives

\[\begin{aligned}\|\mathcal P_N\|&\le C_\infty(R)\mathsf S_N(q),\\\|\mathcal J_\infty^{R\to r}-\mathcal P_N\|&\le C_\infty(R)\mathsf T_N(q).\end{aligned}\]

These operator norms run from \(H^\infty(E_R)\) to \(H^2(E_r)\). The norm-one restriction statement does not make the real-line projection a norm-one map. The exact quantities here are the scalar sums, while the displayed projection estimates are upper bounds.

Neumann reconstruction identifies the discarded series starting at \(n=N\). Its tail tends to zero for the strict gap \(r<R\). Finite-rank approximation then proves compactness, which is a separate conclusion from the norm-one inequality.

Connection to the thesis

This follows “The generic embedding \(\mathcal J_\infty^{R\to r}:H^\infty(E_R)\hookrightarrow H^2(E_r)\)” in the Distilled section “Two Hardy embeddings and Legendre projection tails.” The sharp restriction norm is a thesis sharpening. The exact scalar tail refines the earlier coarse projection estimate.

This is the output restriction in the transfer factorisation. For the later space \(X=H^2(E_r)\), Hardy best approximation allows

\[\|(I-\mathcal P_N^X)\mathcal J_\infty f\|_X\le\|\mathcal J_\infty f-\mathcal P_Nf\|_X.\]

Indeed, \(\mathcal P_Nf\) belongs to the retained polynomial space. This output comparison is valid even though \(\mathcal P_N\) and \(\mathcal P_N^X\) differ. The next chapter refines and packages the output tail with the single-space input bound.
