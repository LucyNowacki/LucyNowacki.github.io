0:00 Straighten the contour contributions. Follow four successive arcs of the inner ellipse. For each arc, integrate the complex density, including the oriented tangent. In the output plane each coloured arrow is one such increment. Join them head to tail. The displacement from start to finish gives the coefficient. Now turn the arrows until they all face right, keeping their lengths. The straightened path can be longer because the original arrows partly cancel. Splitting into finer arcs gives the integral of the absolute density as an upper bound. Inspect the kernel estimate

0:32 Replace the local heights by ceilings. At each boundary point the absolute density contains three factors. The first is the modulus of the function. Its height on the inner rim stays below the outer supremum, M rho. The second is the modulus of the kernel. Bound every height by the largest kernel height on this inner rim. The third is the speed of the moving point. Integrating that speed over one turn gives the perimeter. Those three pieces give the estimate on the screen. Inspect the kernel estimate

1:01 Every second frequency survives. Look inside the kernel at degree three. The first three frequencies are four, six and eight. At one moving point each coloured ridge gives the real part of its wave. The matching arrow carries the whole complex value. Its length is sigma times s to the minus k. A full turn rotates the fourth frequency four times clockwise. All three arrows have fixed lengths at this radius. Their sum changes as their directions change. To bound it at every angle, add their lengths and include the rest of the ladder. Find the kernel waves in Lesson 5

1:32 Sum the geometric majorant. For general degree n, the first frequency is n plus one. Replace each nonnegative sigma by its upper bound of two. Successive upper bounds have ratio one over s squared, since the exponents differ by two. The first upper bound divided by one minus that ratio sums the entire geometric series. Simplify the fraction to get two times s to the minus n divided by s minus its reciprocal. Insert this kernel ceiling into the contour estimate. The two cancels the two in the contour normalization. What remains is the perimeter constant, the function norm, and the geometric decay. Inspect the kernel estimate
