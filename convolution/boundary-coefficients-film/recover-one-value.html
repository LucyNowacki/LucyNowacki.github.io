Recover one real-line value

0:00 Can a loop recover one value? Take the function omega squared. Our target is its value at x equal to one half, namely one quarter. Cauchy's formula recovers this number from a counterclockwise circuit.

0:12 Place a pole at the target. Divide the function by omega minus x. The denominator vanishes at one half, while the numerator there is one quarter. This gives a genuine simple pole.

0:23 Start with a small circle. Its centre is x. Take its radius epsilon to be one quarter for the drawing. Any sufficiently small positive radius works if the circle and its interior lie inside the ellipse.

0:37 The tangent cancels the denominator. On the circle, omega minus x is epsilon times e to the i t. The differential contributes i times that same factor times d t. After dividing by two pi i, only the function value times d t over two pi remains.

0:55 Keep the constant part. Expand the square along the circle. One term is constant, one turns once, and another turns twice. Both rotating terms average to zero over a full turn. What survives is x squared, or one quarter.
