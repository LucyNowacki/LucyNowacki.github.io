Why cos t?

Why cosine? A circle point gives a real Legendre input.

Mark z on the unit circle at angle t. Its reciprocal is its mirror image across the real axis.

Join the two points. Their midpoint keeps their common horizontal coordinate, x equals cosine t.

The midpoint sweeps the interval. Turn from zero to pi, sweeping x from one to minus one.

Stop at ninety degrees. The input is zero. The quadratic Legendre polynomial takes the value minus one half there.

Read the graph. The input is zero and the height is minus one half.

A weighted mean

Give each phase point a mass. Their balance point gives the height of the graph.

Degree two has three phases. Their angles are twice t, zero, and minus twice t.

The weights add to one. Three eighths, one quarter, and three eighths are all positive.

Set t to sixty degrees. The three phase points form a triangle. Their weighted mean is its balance point.

The two mirror points have equal mass. Their imaginary parts cancel, so the balance point sits on the real axis.

Read the balance point. At x equal to one half, P two takes the value minus one eighth.

Why exactly 1?

Why exactly one? Can a Legendre mode exceed one in absolute value?

Every phase has modulus one. Each carries a positive weight, in every degree.

Normalization gives the total weight. At angle zero, all phases equal one. The weights sum to P sub n of one, which is one.

The disk is convex. It contains every weighted mean of these points, so the absolute value is at most one.

The bound covers the whole interval. Every x from minus one to one is cosine t for some angle.

Now line up the phases at angle zero. The mean reaches one at the right endpoint. The supremum is exactly one, in every degree.

The angular bound

Start with a sine wave. Multiply it by the Legendre value, and take k to be a positive integer.

Sigma is a signed integral. Integrate P sub n of cosine t times sine of k t, from zero to pi.

The Legendre factor has magnitude at most one. The product stays between the upper and lower absolute sine envelopes.

Positive and negative areas can cancel. The magnitude of the signed integral is at most the integral of the absolute product.

Look at k equal to three. The upper envelope has three lobes, each of width pi over three and area two thirds.

Count the lobes. For every positive integer k, the k lobes have area two over k each, totaling two.

Every coefficient has magnitude at most two. This holds for every Legendre degree and every positive integer frequency.
